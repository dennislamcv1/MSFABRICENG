1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
==============================================================================================


1.1 Purpose
-----------

This document defines the target architecture for a clinical operations analytics platform
built on Microsoft Fabric for a regional healthcare network of twelve hospitals. It specifies
the data architecture, security and governance model, capacity plan, deployment process, and
operational procedures required to move the network from fragmented, hospital-by-hospital
reporting to a single governed analytics platform that satisfies HIPAA obligations for
Protected Health Information (PHI).

The document is written to be actionable: every section states a specific recommendation with
the reasoning behind it, so that engineering can build from it and the Architecture Review
Board, Privacy Officer, and Information Security Officer can approve or challenge concrete
decisions rather than generalities.


1.2 The Clinical Operations Problem
-----------------------------------

The network currently produces operational reporting through hospital-local extracts from the
Electronic Health Record (EHR), maintained largely in spreadsheets by individual
decision-support analysts. Three consequences follow. First, the same measure is calculated
differently at different hospitals — average length of stay is computed on discharges at some
sites and on census days at others — so network-level comparison is unreliable. Second,
reporting lags the clinical event by seven to ten days, which is too slow to influence
throughput decisions. Third, extracts containing identifiable patient data circulate as files
with no enforceable access control, which is a direct HIPAA exposure.

The platform is designed to answer a defined set of clinical operations questions consistently
across all twelve hospitals:

  * Length of stay: What is the average and median length of stay by hospital, department,
    service line, and discharging provider, and how does it trend against the
    case-mix-adjusted target?
  * Readmissions: Which departments and service lines exceed the network benchmark for 30-day
    unplanned readmission, and which index conditions drive that variance?
  * Encounter volume and demand: What is encounter volume by department, day of week, and hour
    of arrival, and where does arriving demand exceed staffed capacity?
  * Emergency throughput: What is door-to-provider time, ED length of stay, boarding time for
    admitted patients, and left-without-being-seen rate by site?
  * Capacity and flow: What is current bed occupancy and pending-discharge volume, and how
    does discharge timing distribute across the day?
  * Disposition and continuity: What is the discharge disposition mix,
    observation-to-inpatient conversion rate, and rate of discharge against medical advice?
These questions define the scope of the gold layer and the certified semantic model. Any
measure not traceable to one of them is out of scope for release 1.


1.3 Solution Approach
---------------------

The platform is built entirely on Microsoft Fabric, using OneLake as the single storage layer
and a Medallion (bronze / silver / gold) architecture to separate raw capture from clinical
standardization and from analytics-ready aggregation. Patient encounter data is ingested daily
from the EHR by Data Factory pipelines invoking watermark-driven Copy activity; a parallel
Eventstream carries HL7 Admit-Discharge-Transfer (ADT) messages into an Eventhouse for
near-real-time census and bed-occupancy visibility. PySpark notebooks perform cleansing,
terminology mapping, and deduplication in silver, and build conformed dimensional and
aggregate tables in gold.

A single certified semantic model sits on gold in Direct Lake mode and carries the
network-approved definitions of every clinical KPI. Security is layered: Microsoft Entra ID
groups drive Fabric workspace roles; row-level security (RLS) restricts each user to their own
hospital, department, or provider panel; object-level and column-level security (OLS/CLS)
remove PHI columns from any role that does not need identifiable data; and explicit SQL GRANT
and DENY statements govern direct query access through the Lakehouse SQL analytics endpoint.
All content is version-controlled in Git and promoted through Development, Test, and
Production workspaces by Fabric deployment pipelines with environment-specific deployment
rules, triggered on pull request completion.


1.4 Key Benefits
----------------

Four benefits justify the investment and should be the basis for measuring the platform after
go-live.

   1. Faster access to clinical data. Encounter data is available to department leadership by
      06:00 the morning after the clinical day, replacing a seven-to-ten-day reporting lag, and
      ED census and bed occupancy refresh within five minutes of the underlying ADT event.
      Operational decisions about staffing and throughput can be made on the current day rather
      than retrospectively.
   2. Consistent metrics across all twelve hospitals. One certified semantic model holds one
      definition of average length of stay, readmission rate, and encounter volume. Site-level
      comparison, service-line review, and board reporting all draw from the same measures,
      which removes the recurring reconciliation argument that currently consumes
      decision-support capacity.
   3. Demonstrable HIPAA compliance. Minimum-necessary access is enforced technically rather
      than by policy alone: RLS confines users to their own department or panel, OLS and CLS
      strip identifiers from analyst-facing roles, and every access to identifiable data is
      logged and retained. The network can produce evidence of who accessed which patient data,
      when, and under what authorization — evidence that spreadsheet distribution cannot
      produce.
   4. Lower total cost and faster delivery of new analytics. Consolidating hospital-local
      extracts onto a single Fabric capacity removes duplicated storage and duplicated
      engineering effort. At the F64 capacity recommended in Section 4, report consumers do not
      require individual Power BI Pro licenses, which materially reduces the cost of extending
      access from decision-support staff to frontline clinical leadership.

1.6 Out of Scope for Release 1
------------------------------

  * Clinical quality registry submission, regulatory quality measure certification (for
    example eCQM abstraction), and payer claims analytics.
  * Predictive or machine-learning models, including readmission risk scoring. The gold layer
    is designed to support these later, but no model is deployed in release 1.
  * Revenue cycle, supply chain, and human resources subject areas.
  * Write-back to the EHR. The platform is strictly read-only with respect to clinical source
    systems.
  * Patient-facing or externally shared reporting. All distribution is internal to the network
    tenant.

2.3 Data Flow: Sources to Reports
---------------------------------

The following walkthrough describes Figure 1 in narrative form.

   1. Extraction. At 01:00 local time, pipeline pl_ehr_daily_load starts. A Copy Job reads
      changed rows from the EHR clinical data warehouse using a high-water mark on the source
      last-modified timestamp, stored in a control table in lh_bronze. Each of the twelve
      hospitals is a partition of the same logical extract; the pipeline uses a ForEach over
      the hospital list with a bounded concurrency of four to avoid saturating the source
      system during its own maintenance window.
   2. Bronze landing. Rows land in lh_bronze as Delta tables, appended without modification and
      stamped with technical audit columns: source system, extract batch identifier, source
      file or query fingerprint, and ingestion timestamp. Bronze is never edited or corrected
      in place. If a source correction arrives, it lands as a new row, and silver resolves
      precedence. This preserves an accurate record of what the EHR asserted at each point in
      time, which matters both for clinical defensibility and for HIPAA audit.
   3. Silver standardization. PySpark notebooks read new bronze partitions and write conformed
      tables to lh_silver. This layer performs date and time normalization to a single time
      zone convention, terminology mapping to standard code sets, deduplication to one row per
      encounter using the enterprise patient identifier and source encounter identifier,
      referential validation against dimension sources, and rejection of records failing
      mandatory-field or plausibility checks into a quarantine table for steward review.
   4. Gold modelling. A second notebook set builds the conformed star schema in lh_gold —
      dimensions for patient, provider, department, hospital, date, diagnosis, and disposition,
      and fact tables at encounter grain plus daily census grain — and then materializes the
      pre-aggregated KPI tables that back the highest-traffic report pages. Identifiable
      patient attributes are written to a separate restricted schema (referred to in this
      document as the PHI vault) rather than being mixed into the general dimension tables.
   5. Semantic model refresh. The certified semantic model sm_clinical_ops sits directly on
      gold Delta tables in Direct Lake mode, so no data copy or scheduled import refresh is
      required; the pipeline issues a framing operation at the end of the run so the model
      picks up the new Delta version. RLS roles and OLS rules are defined in the model and
      evaluated per user at query time.
   6. Consumption. Power BI reports connect live to the certified model. A separate real-time
      dashboard queries the Eventhouse for live census. Activator rules watch both paths and
      raise alerts to Teams and email when thresholds are crossed — including a data-freshness
      rule that fires if gold has not been updated by 06:00.
   7. Notification and close-out. The pipeline writes run statistics to an operational log
      table, posts a completion summary to the data engineering Teams channel, and, on failure,
      raises an incident through the process defined in Section 6.2.

Recommendation: a Data Factory pipeline orchestrates the nightly run; Copy Job performs the
incremental EHR extract into bronze; PySpark notebooks perform all transformation; Dataflow
Gen2 is permitted only for low-volume reference data maintained by clinical informatics;
Eventstream serves the real-time ADT path. This split gives engineering-grade control over the
clinical data path while still allowing informatics analysts to maintain mapping tables
without filing engineering tickets.

Incremental strategy: the extract is watermark-based on the source last-modified timestamp,
with a deliberate 48-hour lookback window on each run. Clinical source systems routinely
backdate corrections — a coding update or a late discharge disposition entered two days after
the fact — and a naive high-water mark would silently miss them. Silver applies
last-write-wins precedence within the lookback window, so restated records correct themselves
without a full reload.


2.5 Medallion Layer Design for Clinical Data
--------------------------------------------


Bronze — raw clinical capture

Bronze is an append-only, schema-on-read reproduction of what the source supplied, with no
business logic applied. Tables are partitioned by ingestion date. Retention is seven years to
align with the network medical record retention policy, with data older than thirteen months
moved to cool storage. Bronze is accessible only to the data engineering service principal and
to break-glass administrators; no analyst and no report ever reads bronze.

Silver — conformed clinical data

Silver applies the standardization rules that make twelve hospitals comparable. The specific
transformations are:

  * Date and time normalization. All timestamps are stored in Coordinated Universal Time with
    a companion local-time column and the site time zone, so overnight boundaries and
    daylight-saving transitions do not distort length-of-stay or arrival-hour analysis.
  * Terminology mapping. Site-local codes for department, encounter class, discharge
    disposition, and admission source are mapped to the network standard value set. Unmapped
    codes are routed to a quarantine table and reported daily to clinical informatics rather
    than silently defaulted, because a silent default here corrupts the very comparisons the
    platform exists to make.
  * Deduplication. One row per encounter is retained, keyed on enterprise patient identifier
    plus source encounter identifier, resolving to the most recent source version within the
    lookback window.
  * Patient identity resolution. The EMPI enterprise identifier is attached to every encounter
    so that a patient treated at Hospital A and readmitted to Hospital B is recognized as one
    person — a prerequisite for network-level readmission measurement.
  * Clinical validity checks. Discharge before admission, negative length of stay, implausible
    ages, and encounters referencing unknown departments are rejected to quarantine with a
    reason code.
  * PHI segregation begins here. Direct identifiers are written to a restricted silver schema,
    and downstream tables carry surrogate keys, so the default path for any consumer is
    de-identified.

census date for the snapshot fact.


2.6 Semantic Model Design
-------------------------

One certified semantic model, sm_clinical_ops, serves all reports. It is deliberately
singular: multiple models would reintroduce exactly the metric divergence the platform exists
to eliminate. The model is configured in Direct Lake mode over gold, with a fallback to
DirectQuery disabled so that a silent fallback cannot degrade performance without anyone
noticing.

Model structure follows a strict star: fact tables related to dimensions on surrogate keys,
single-direction filters from dimension to fact, no bidirectional relationships except through
the security bridge described in Section 3.5, and no calculated columns on fact tables.
Representative measure definitions:


    Discharges =
        CALCULATE(
            COUNTROWS( fact_encounter ),
            fact_encounter[EncounterStatus] = "Discharged",
            fact_encounter[EncounterClass] IN { "Inpatient", "Observation" }
        )
    
    Patient Days =
        SUM( fact_encounter[LengthOfStayDays] )
    
    Average Length of Stay =
        DIVIDE( [Patient Days], [Discharges] )
    
    Index Discharges =
        CALCULATE(
            COUNTROWS( fact_readmission ),
            fact_readmission[IsEligibleIndex] = TRUE()
        )
    
    30-Day Unplanned Readmissions =
        CALCULATE(
            COUNTROWS( fact_readmission ),
            fact_readmission[IsEligibleIndex] = TRUE(),
            fact_readmission[ReadmitWithin30Days] = TRUE(),
            fact_readmission[IsPlannedReadmission] = FALSE()
        )
    
    30-Day Readmission Rate =
        DIVIDE( [30-Day Unplanned Readmissions], [Index Discharges] )
    
    Encounter Volume =
        COUNTROWS( fact_encounter )
    
    Occupancy Rate =
        DIVIDE(
            SUM( fact_daily_census[MidnightCensus] ),
            SUM( fact_daily_census[StaffedBeds] )
        )
    
    -- Small-cell suppression: never display a rate derived from fewer than
    -- 11 encounters, to prevent re-identification in thinly populated
    -- department-by-month cells.
    30-Day Readmission Rate (Safe) =
        IF( [Index Discharges] < 11, BLANK(), [30-Day Readmission Rate] )

The small-cell suppression pattern shown in the final measure is applied to every rate measure
exposed at department, provider, or month grain. A readmission rate computed on four
encounters is both clinically meaningless and a genuine re-identification risk in a small
specialty department, and suppression at the measure layer is more reliable than asking report
authors to remember.


2.7 Real-Time Component
-----------------------

Length of stay and readmission are retrospective measures and are well served by the nightly
batch. Bed occupancy and ED boarding are not — a house supervisor making a bed assignment at
14:00 cannot use last night data. A real-time path is therefore in scope for release 1, but is
deliberately narrow.

  * Ingestion: the existing integration engine forwards a copy of HL7 v2 ADT messages (A01
    admit, A02 transfer, A03 discharge, A08 update, A11/A13 cancellations) to an Eventstream,
    which parses them to JSON.
  * Storage: events land in Eventhouse (KQL database) eh_clinical_rt with a 90-day hot
    retention, sufficient for operational use; the authoritative long-term record remains the
    batch path.
  * Consumption: a Real-Time Dashboard shows live census by unit,
    admitted-patients-awaiting-bed count, and boarding duration. Activator rules raise alerts
    to the house supervisor Teams channel when boarding time exceeds four hours or a unit
    exceeds ninety-five percent occupancy.
  * Reconciliation: the real-time path is explicitly labelled as operational and
    non-authoritative. Every real-time dashboard carries a footer stating that figures are
    provisional and that the certified batch model is the system of record for reported
    metrics. A daily reconciliation job compares real-time census against the batch snapshot
    and flags variance above two percent for investigation.
The same PHI rules apply to the real-time path. The Eventhouse holds the enterprise patient
identifier but not patient name or date of birth; the dashboard displays bed and unit-level
counts rather than patient-level detail.

3. SECURITY AND GOVERNANCE
==============================================================================================


3.1 Security Design Principles
------------------------------

The security model is built on the assumption that a single control will eventually fail, and
that no single control should be the only thing standing between a user and identifiable
patient data. Five layers apply in sequence, and a user must pass all of them to see a value.


Layer    | Control                        | What It Restricts
---------+--------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------
1        | Microsoft Entra ID group       | Whether the person can authenticate to the platform
         | membership                     | at all
2        | Fabric workspace role          | Which items the person can see, edit, or publish
3        | Row-level security (RLS)       | Which patient encounter rows the person can
         |                                | retrieve
4        | Object- and column-level       | Which PHI attributes exist for that person at all
         | security (OLS/CLS)             |
5        | SQL GRANT / DENY on the SQL    | Whether direct T-SQL query bypasses the semantic
         | analytics endpoint             | model

Two rules govern the whole design. First, deny by default: a new user receives no access until
assigned to a group, and a new PHI column is inaccessible until explicitly granted. Second, no
standing broad access: nobody holds network-wide identifiable data access as a routine
entitlement; that access is time-bound, justified, and logged.


3.2 Identity and Group Structure
--------------------------------

All access is granted to Microsoft Entra ID security groups, never to individual users. Groups
are populated from the HR and provider credentialing systems where possible, so that a
transfer or termination propagates automatically. Individual assignment is permitted only for
break-glass administrator accounts, which are separately monitored.

-----------------------------+-------------------------------+-------------------------------
SG-Fabric-Clinical-Admins    | Platform architects (3 named) | Workspace Admin, production
SG-Fabric-Clinical-Engineers | Data engineering team (8)     | Contributor in dev/test, none
                             |                               | in prod
SG-Fabric-Clinical-Analysts  | Decision support analysts     | Viewer, de-identified network
                             | (26)                          | scope
SG-Fabric-Clinical-DeptHeads | Department heads and managers | Viewer, RLS-scoped to own
                             | (140)                         | department

                             |                               | panel
                             | Hospital and network          | Viewer, network scope,

                             | Privacy and compliance        | Viewer with audit access,
SG-Fabric-Clinical-Compliance | officers (6)                  | PHI-eligible
                             | Automation identities (4)     | Contributor via service

                             | Emergency access accounts (2) | Admin, PIM-activated, 4-hour

Privileged roles — workspace Admin and any PHI-eligible role — are assigned through Microsoft
Entra Privileged Identity Management with activation requiring justification, approval, and
multi-factor authentication, and a maximum activation duration of four hours. Standing
membership in these roles is prohibited.


3.3 Workspace Roles and Assignments
-----------------------------------

Fabric provides four workspace roles. Their practical meaning for this platform is:

  * Admin — full control including workspace deletion, access management, and capacity
    assignment. Restricted to three named platform architects, activated through PIM.
  * Member — can add other users up to Member level and manage all content. Deliberately
    unused in production, because it allows access re-granting outside the change process.
  * Contributor — can create and modify items but cannot manage access. This is the working
    role for engineers in development and test.
  * Viewer — can read reports and query the semantic model, subject to RLS and OLS. This is
    the role for every clinical consumer.

granting them Admin would weaken the very separation of duties they exist to verify.


3.3.1 Item-level and OneLake permissions

Workspace roles are supplemented by item-level sharing so that Viewer in the production
workspace does not become a back door to raw data. Specifically: lh_bronze and lh_silver have
OneLake data access removed for the Viewer role, so a workspace Viewer cannot open the bronze
Lakehouse or query its SQL endpoint; lh_gold grants read only through the semantic model, not
through direct file access; and the semantic model is shared with the Build permission
required for report consumption but not the write permission that would allow a user to alter
measure definitions.

3.5 Row-Level Security Design
-----------------------------

RLS is dynamic and data-driven. Rather than creating one static role per department — which
would mean maintaining 60 roles across 12 hospitals — a single security bridge table maps each
user principal name to the scopes they are entitled to, and a small number of DAX roles read
that table at query time.


3.5.1 The security scope table

Table sec_user_scope is built nightly in gold from the HR system, the provider credentialing
system, and the department registry. It is the single source of truth for who sees what.


Column                | Type             | Description
----------------------+------------------+--------------------------------------------------
UserPrincipalName     | string           | Entra ID UPN, lower-cased, the join key to
                      |                  | USERPRINCIPALNAME()
ScopeType             | string           | One of Department, Hospital, Panel, Network
HospitalKey           | int              | Surrogate key of the hospital in scope; -1 for
                      |                  | network scope
DepartmentKey         | int              | Surrogate key of the department in scope; -1 for
                      |                  | hospital or network scope
ProviderKey           | int              | Surrogate key of the provider whose panel is in
                      |                  | scope; -1 if not panel-scoped
EffectiveFrom /       | date             | Supports scheduled onboarding and automatic
EffectiveTo           |                  | expiry on transfer or termination
GrantedBy /           | string           | Retained for HIPAA audit: who authorized this
GrantReason           |                  | access and why

The table is filtered to rows where the current date falls between EffectiveFrom and
EffectiveTo, so a department head who moves to another service loses the old scope
automatically on the effective date rather than waiting for a manual review.

3.5.3 DAX filter expressions

Filters are applied to the dimension tables, and propagate to facts through the model
relationships. Applying the predicate to dim_department rather than to fact_encounter is
deliberate: the dimension is small, the filter evaluates once, and every fact related to that
dimension inherits the restriction, so a new fact table added later is protected by default.


    -- Role: RLS_DepartmentHead
    -- Applied to table: dim_department
    VAR CurrentUser = LOWER( USERPRINCIPALNAME() )
    RETURN
        dim_department[DepartmentKey] IN
            SELECTCOLUMNS(
                FILTER(
                    sec_user_scope,
                    LOWER( sec_user_scope[UserPrincipalName] ) = CurrentUser
                        && sec_user_scope[ScopeType] = "Department"
                        && sec_user_scope[EffectiveFrom] <= TODAY()
                        && sec_user_scope[EffectiveTo]   >= TODAY()
                ),
                "DeptKey", sec_user_scope[DepartmentKey]
            )
    
    -- Role: RLS_HospitalLeader
    -- Applied to table: dim_hospital
    VAR CurrentUser = LOWER( USERPRINCIPALNAME() )
    RETURN
        dim_hospital[HospitalKey] IN
            SELECTCOLUMNS(
                FILTER(
                    sec_user_scope,
                    LOWER( sec_user_scope[UserPrincipalName] ) = CurrentUser
                        && sec_user_scope[ScopeType] = "Hospital"
                        && sec_user_scope[EffectiveFrom] <= TODAY()
                        && sec_user_scope[EffectiveTo]   >= TODAY()
                ),
                "HospKey", sec_user_scope[HospitalKey]
            )
    
    -- Role: RLS_Provider
    -- Applied to table: dim_provider
    VAR CurrentUser = LOWER( USERPRINCIPALNAME() )
    RETURN
        dim_provider[ProviderKey] IN
            SELECTCOLUMNS(
                FILTER(
                    sec_user_scope,
                    LOWER( sec_user_scope[UserPrincipalName] ) = CurrentUser
                        && sec_user_scope[ScopeType] = "Panel"
                        && sec_user_scope[EffectiveFrom] <= TODAY()
                        && sec_user_scope[EffectiveTo]   >= TODAY()
                ),
                "ProvKey", sec_user_scope[ProviderKey]
            )
    
    -- Roles: RLS_NetworkAnalyst, RLS_Executive, RLS_Compliance
    -- Applied to table: sec_user_scope (guard filter only)
    -- No filter is placed on dim_department, dim_hospital or dim_provider,
    -- so all rows are visible. The guard below still requires the user to
    -- hold an active Network scope row, so a lapsed or revoked entitlement
    -- returns an empty result set rather than the whole network.
    VAR CurrentUser = LOWER( USERPRINCIPALNAME() )
    RETURN
        CALCULATE(
            COUNTROWS( sec_user_scope ),
            LOWER( sec_user_scope[UserPrincipalName] ) = CurrentUser,
            sec_user_scope[ScopeType] = "Network",
            sec_user_scope[EffectiveFrom] <= TODAY(),
            sec_user_scope[EffectiveTo]   >= TODAY()
        ) > 0

Worked example. A Cardiology department head at Hospital 04 holds one row in sec_user_scope
with ScopeType Department and the DepartmentKey for Cardiology at Hospital 04. When she opens
the length-of-stay report, the RLS_DepartmentHead filter reduces dim_department to that one
row; the relationship to fact_encounter propagates the restriction; and every visual on the
page — including the network comparison tile — returns only Cardiology encounters at her site.
If she is also given oversight of Cardiac Surgery, a second row is added to sec_user_scope and
both departments appear the following day with no model change and no redeployment.


3.5.4 RLS implementation rules

  * Users must never be workspace Admin, Member, or Contributor in the production workspace,
    because those roles bypass RLS entirely. This is the single most common cause of RLS
    failure in practice, and the role table in Section 3.3 is written to prevent it.
  * The sec_user_scope table itself is hidden from report view and protected by its own filter
    so that a user cannot read the entitlements of others.
  * A user with no matching scope row sees no data, not all data. This fail-closed behaviour
    is verified by an automated test in the deployment pipeline.
  * Every RLS role is validated after each deployment using the View as role function plus a
    scripted DAX query executed as a set of test principals, with expected row counts
    asserted. Section 5.7 defines this as a release gate.

3.6 Object-Level and Column-Level Security for PHI
--------------------------------------------------

RLS controls which rows a user sees. It does nothing about which columns. A department head
correctly restricted to Cardiology encounters would still see patient name on every one of
those rows unless a second control is applied. OLS in the semantic model and CLS at the SQL
endpoint provide that control.


3.6.1 Object-level security in the semantic model

OLS is defined per role and applied to the PHI vault table and its columns. When a column is
OLS-restricted, it does not merely return blank — it is absent from the metadata for that
user, so it cannot be selected, referenced in a measure, or discovered through field-list
exploration. Any visual that references a restricted column returns an error for that role
rather than silently exposing data, so OLS-restricted fields are kept off shared report pages
and placed on role-specific pages.


Role                     | PatientName    |                    | DateOfBirth | AgeBand
                         |                | MedicalRecordNumber |             |
-------------------------+----------------+--------------------+-------------+--------------
RLS_Compliance           | Visible        | Visible            | Visible     | Visible
RLS_Provider             | Hidden         | Visible (own       | Not in      | Visible
                         |                | panel)             | model       |
RLS_DepartmentHead       | Hidden         | Hidden             | Not in      | Visible
                         |                |                    | model       |
RLS_HospitalLeader       | Hidden         | Hidden             | Not in      | Visible
                         |                |                    | model       |
RLS_NetworkAnalyst       | Hidden         | Hidden             | Not in      | Visible
                         |                |                    | model       |
RLS_Executive            | Hidden         | Hidden             | Not in      | Visible
                         |                |                    | model       |

The provider exception is intentional and clinically necessary: a physician reviewing her own
panel needs the medical record number to act on a finding, and she already has
treatment-relationship access to those same patients in the EHR. Extending it no further than
her own panel is exactly the minimum-necessary standard applied correctly.

OLS is expressed in the model metadata. The TMSL fragment below shows the pattern applied to
the department head role; it is generated and applied by script during deployment rather than
clicked in a user interface, so it is version-controlled and reviewable.


    {
      "roles": [
        {
          "name": "RLS_DepartmentHead",
          "modelPermission": "read",
          "tablePermissions": [
            {
              "name": "dim_patient_vault",
              "metadataPermission": "none"
            },
            {
              "name": "dim_patient",
              "columnPermissions": [
                { "name": "PatientName",          "metadataPermission": "none" },
                { "name": "MedicalRecordNumber",  "metadataPermission": "none" }
              ]
            }
          ]
        }
      ]
    }


3.6.2 Column-level security and masking at the SQL endpoint

OLS protects the semantic model. It does not protect a user who connects a SQL client directly
to the Lakehouse SQL analytics endpoint. CLS and dynamic data masking close that path.


    -- Column-level security: grant only the columns a role needs.
    -- Granting at column level implicitly denies the ungranted columns.
    
    GRANT SELECT ON gold.dim_patient
        ( PatientKey, AgeAtEncounter, AgeBand, SexAtBirth,
          ZipCode3, PrimaryLanguage )
        TO [SG-Fabric-Clinical-DeptHeads];
    
    GRANT SELECT ON gold.dim_patient
        ( PatientKey, AgeAtEncounter, AgeBand, SexAtBirth,
          ZipCode3, PrimaryLanguage, MedicalRecordNumber )
        TO [SG-Fabric-Clinical-Providers];
    
    -- Compliance receives the full column set, including direct identifiers.
    GRANT SELECT ON gold.dim_patient_vault
        TO [SG-Fabric-Clinical-Compliance];
    
    -- Dynamic data masking as a second line of defence, so that a
    -- misconfigured grant still does not surface a usable identifier.
    ALTER TABLE gold.dim_patient_vault
        ALTER COLUMN MedicalRecordNumber
        ADD MASKED WITH (FUNCTION = 'partial(0,"XXXXXX",4)');
    
    ALTER TABLE gold.dim_patient_vault
        ALTER COLUMN PatientName
        ADD MASKED WITH (FUNCTION = 'default()');
    
    GRANT UNMASK TO [SG-Fabric-Clinical-Compliance];

Row-level security is also enforced at the SQL endpoint, so that the department restriction
holds for direct query users exactly as it does in the semantic model. Without this, a
department head with SQL access could simply query around the model.


    CREATE SCHEMA sec;
    GO
    
    CREATE FUNCTION sec.fn_department_predicate(@DepartmentKey INT)
        RETURNS TABLE
        WITH SCHEMABINDING
    AS
    RETURN
        SELECT 1 AS fn_result
        WHERE EXISTS (
            SELECT 1
            FROM   gold.sec_user_scope AS s
            WHERE  s.UserPrincipalName = LOWER(USER_NAME())
              AND  s.ScopeType         = 'Department'
              AND  s.DepartmentKey     = @DepartmentKey
              AND  CAST(GETUTCDATE() AS DATE)
                   BETWEEN s.EffectiveFrom AND s.EffectiveTo
        )
        OR IS_ROLEMEMBER('db_network_wide_read') = 1;
    GO
    
    CREATE SECURITY POLICY sec.encounter_department_filter
        ADD FILTER PREDICATE sec.fn_department_predicate(DepartmentKey)
            ON gold.fact_encounter,
        ADD FILTER PREDICATE sec.fn_department_predicate(DepartmentKey)
            ON gold.fact_daily_census,
        ADD FILTER PREDICATE sec.fn_department_predicate(DepartmentKey)
            ON gold.fact_ed_throughput
    WITH (STATE = ON);
    GO


3.7 SQL GRANT and DENY Statements
---------------------------------

The statements below govern the Lakehouse SQL analytics endpoint and Warehouse. They
complement — and must not contradict — the workspace roles in Section 3.3. Where the two
overlap, the more restrictive outcome must prevail, and Section 3.7.2 defines the audit that
verifies this.


3.7.1 Grant and deny script


    --------------------------------------------------------------------
    -- 1. Database roles: grant to roles, never to individual users.
    --------------------------------------------------------------------
    CREATE ROLE db_clinical_read_deid;      -- de-identified analytics
    CREATE ROLE db_clinical_read_phi;       -- identifiable, restricted
    CREATE ROLE db_network_wide_read;       -- bypasses department RLS
    CREATE ROLE db_etl_service;             -- automation identities only
    CREATE ROLE db_audit_read;              -- compliance, logs only
    GO
    
    ALTER ROLE db_clinical_read_deid ADD MEMBER [SG-Fabric-Clinical-Analysts];
    ALTER ROLE db_clinical_read_deid ADD MEMBER [SG-Fabric-Clinical-DeptHeads];
    ALTER ROLE db_network_wide_read  ADD MEMBER [SG-Fabric-Clinical-Analysts];
    ALTER ROLE db_clinical_read_phi  ADD MEMBER [SG-Fabric-Clinical-Compliance];
    ALTER ROLE db_audit_read         ADD MEMBER [SG-Fabric-Clinical-Compliance];
    ALTER ROLE db_etl_service        ADD MEMBER [svc-fabric-clinical-etl];
    GO
    
    --------------------------------------------------------------------
    -- 2. Baseline: no access to anything outside gold.
    --------------------------------------------------------------------
    DENY SELECT ON SCHEMA::bronze TO db_clinical_read_deid,
                                     db_clinical_read_phi,
                                     db_network_wide_read,
                                     db_audit_read;
    
    DENY SELECT ON SCHEMA::silver TO db_clinical_read_deid,
                                     db_clinical_read_phi,
                                     db_network_wide_read,
                                     db_audit_read;
    GO
    
    --------------------------------------------------------------------
    -- 3. De-identified analytics: read gold facts and dimensions,
    --    but never the PHI vault.
    --------------------------------------------------------------------
    GRANT SELECT ON SCHEMA::gold          TO db_clinical_read_deid;
    DENY  SELECT ON gold.dim_patient_vault TO db_clinical_read_deid;
    DENY  SELECT ON gold.sec_user_scope    TO db_clinical_read_deid;
    
    -- Belt and braces: deny the specific identifier columns even if a
    -- future schema change surfaces them in a general gold table.
    DENY SELECT ON gold.dim_patient ( PatientName, MedicalRecordNumber,
                                      DateOfBirth, SocialSecurityNumber )
         TO db_clinical_read_deid;
    GO
    
    --------------------------------------------------------------------
    -- 4. PHI-eligible access: compliance and privacy investigation only.
    --------------------------------------------------------------------
    GRANT SELECT ON SCHEMA::gold           TO db_clinical_read_phi;
    GRANT SELECT ON gold.dim_patient_vault TO db_clinical_read_phi;
    GRANT UNMASK                           TO db_clinical_read_phi;
    DENY  INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE ON SCHEMA::gold TO db_clinical_read_phi;
    GO
    
    --------------------------------------------------------------------
    -- 5. ETL service principal: write to gold, read everything upstream,
    --    but no rights to change security objects.
    --------------------------------------------------------------------
    GRANT SELECT                          ON SCHEMA::bronze TO db_etl_service;
    GRANT SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE  ON SCHEMA::silver TO db_etl_service;
    GRANT SELECT, INSERT, UPDATE, DELETE  ON SCHEMA::gold   TO db_etl_service;
    DENY  ALTER, CONTROL                  ON SCHEMA::sec    TO db_etl_service;
    DENY  ALTER ANY SECURITY POLICY                         TO db_etl_service;
    GO
    
    --------------------------------------------------------------------
    -- 6. Audit access: log tables only, no clinical data.
    --------------------------------------------------------------------
    GRANT SELECT ON SCHEMA::audit TO db_audit_read;
    DENY  SELECT ON SCHEMA::gold  TO db_audit_read;
    GO
    
    --------------------------------------------------------------------
    -- 7. Universal prohibitions.
    --------------------------------------------------------------------
    DENY ALTER ANY USER, ALTER ANY ROLE TO db_clinical_read_deid,
                                           db_clinical_read_phi,
                                           db_network_wide_read,
                                           db_audit_read;
    
    DENY VIEW DEFINITION ON SCHEMA::sec TO db_clinical_read_deid,
                                           db_network_wide_read;
    GO

Two conventions are worth stating explicitly. DENY beats GRANT in SQL Server permission
resolution, which is why the pattern throughout is to grant broadly at schema level and then
deny narrowly at the sensitive object. And permissions are granted to database roles rather
than to Entra groups directly, so that changing what a persona can do is a single role edit
rather than a hunt through scattered object grants.


3.7.2 Reconciling SQL permissions with workspace roles

Workspace roles and SQL grants are two independent permission systems over the same data, and
they can disagree. A workspace Viewer with OneLake data access retained could read gold files
directly regardless of a SQL DENY; conversely a SQL GRANT is meaningless to a user with no
workspace access at all. Three rules resolve this.

   1. The workspace role establishes the ceiling. No SQL GRANT can widen access beyond what the
      workspace role permits, and OneLake data access is removed for Viewer on bronze and
      silver so that file-level reads cannot bypass the SQL layer.
   2. SQL DENY establishes the floor. Where a user holds workspace access that would otherwise
      permit an object, an explicit DENY on that object removes it.
   3. Overlaps are audited, not assumed. A monthly reconciliation job enumerates effective
      permissions from both systems for every Entra group and reports any principal whose
      effective SQL access exceeds its intended persona. The report goes to the Information
      Security Officer and the Privacy Officer. Any discrepancy is treated as a finding with a
      fourteen-day remediation deadline, and unresolved findings escalate to the Architecture
      Review Board.

    -- Monthly effective-permission extract feeding the reconciliation report
    SELECT  pr.name                AS principal_name,
            pr.type_desc           AS principal_type,
            pe.state_desc          AS grant_or_deny,
            pe.permission_name,
            SCHEMA_NAME(o.schema_id) AS schema_name,
            o.name                 AS object_name,
            c.name                 AS column_name
    FROM    sys.database_permissions AS pe
    JOIN    sys.database_principals  AS pr ON pr.principal_id = pe.grantee_principal_id
    LEFT JOIN sys.objects            AS o  ON o.object_id     = pe.major_id
    LEFT JOIN sys.columns            AS c  ON c.object_id     = pe.major_id
                                          AND c.column_id     = pe.minor_id
    WHERE   pr.name LIKE 'db_%' OR pr.name LIKE 'SG-Fabric-%'
    ORDER BY principal_name, schema_name, object_name, column_name;


3.8 HIPAA Compliance Considerations
-----------------------------------


3.8.1 Minimum necessary

The HIPAA minimum necessary standard requires that access be limited to the least PHI needed
to accomplish the purpose. The platform implements this through the layered controls already
described, and the mapping is explicit so it can be evidenced during an audit: role-based
workspace assignment limits who reaches the platform; RLS limits which patients each role can
see; OLS and CLS limit which attributes exist for that role; and de-identification during the
silver-to-gold build means most analytic work never touches identifiers at all. Access
requests are reviewed against a documented justification, recorded in sec_user_scope with
GrantedBy and GrantReason, and recertified quarterly by the relevant department head with a
fourteen-day response window; scopes not recertified expire automatically.


3.8.2 Audit logging

HIPAA requires that the network be able to reconstruct who accessed PHI, when, and what they
did. Four log sources are combined and exported daily to the enterprise SIEM, where they are
retained for six years to satisfy the HIPAA documentation retention requirement.

credentials.


3.8.3 Breach notification procedure

The platform does not create a separate breach process; it feeds the network existing HIPAA
incident process, and this section defines the platform obligations within it.

   1. Detection and containment (target: within 1 hour of detection). The on-call data engineer
      suspends the affected access — removing the group assignment, disabling the security
      policy, or pausing the workspace — and preserves the relevant logs by exporting the
      affected time window to immutable storage before any remediation changes the state.
   2. Notification to the Privacy Officer (within 1 hour). Any suspected impermissible access
      or disclosure of PHI is reported immediately, without waiting for confirmation.
      Under-reporting a false alarm is cheap; late reporting is not.
   3. Risk assessment (within 5 business days). The Privacy Officer conducts the four-factor
      assessment required by the Breach Notification Rule: nature and extent of the PHI
      involved, the unauthorized person who accessed it, whether PHI was actually acquired or
      viewed, and the extent to which risk has been mitigated. The platform supplies the
      evidence: the exact rows and columns returned by the queries in question, reconstructed
      from the query log and the state of the RLS scope table at that time.
   4. Notification (as determined by assessment). If a breach is confirmed, affected
      individuals are notified without unreasonable delay and no later than sixty days from
      discovery; the Secretary of Health and Human Services is notified within sixty days for
      breaches affecting five hundred or more individuals, and through the annual log
      otherwise; media notification applies to breaches affecting more than five hundred
      residents of a state or jurisdiction.
   5. Post-incident review (within 30 days). A root-cause review determines whether a control
      failed or was absent, and produces a tracked remediation item. Where the cause was a
      permission misconfiguration, the reconciliation audit in Section 3.7.2 is extended to
      detect that class of error.

3.8.5 Additional technical safeguards

  * Encryption: data at rest in OneLake is encrypted by the platform; the network additionally
    applies customer-managed keys where supported. Data in transit uses TLS 1.2 or higher on
    every connection, including the EHR extract.
  * Network isolation: capacity access is restricted to the corporate network and managed
    devices through conditional access policy; a private endpoint is used for the EHR source
    connection so extract traffic does not traverse the public internet.
  * Export controls: the tenant-level setting permitting export to Excel and CSV is disabled
    for the clinical workspaces, and Purview sensitivity labels with encryption are applied to
    the semantic model so that any file that is legitimately exported carries protection with
    it.
  * Sharing controls: publish-to-web is disabled tenant-wide; external guest sharing is
    blocked for the clinical workspaces; and reports may not be shared to distribution lists
    that are not backed by a recertified security group.

3.9 Data Governance
-------------------

Governance roles are assigned by name before go-live. The Chief Medical Information Officer
owns KPI definitions and adjudicates disputes about clinical meaning. A named data steward in
clinical informatics owns terminology mappings and the quarantine queue. The Privacy Officer
owns the PHI classification in Section 3.4 and approves any change to it. Data Engineering
owns pipeline correctness and the operational log.

The semantic model is published with the Certified endorsement so that it appears as the
network-approved source in the Fabric data hub; uncertified models built by analysts in the
sandbox workspace remain visibly distinct. Item lineage and impact analysis are reviewed
before any breaking change to a gold table, and the change advisory process in Section 5.8
applies to any change that alters a published KPI definition.

4.2.1 Rationale

   1. Licensing economics dominate. Below the F64 threshold, every consumer needs a Power BI
      Pro license. At roughly 830 named consumers, the annual per-user cost exceeds the
      difference between F32 and F64 several times over. The correct comparison is not F32
      capacity cost against F64 capacity cost, but F32 plus 830 Pro licenses against F64 alone
      — and F64 wins decisively.
   2. The peak, not the average, sizes the capacity. Average daily utilization is projected at
      roughly fifteen to twenty percent of an F64. That number is not the constraint. Monday
      between 07:00 and 09:00, when every department reviews weekend activity simultaneously,
      is the constraint, and F32 would risk throttling exactly when the platform is most
      visible to clinical leadership.
   3. Direct Lake requires memory headroom. Direct Lake loads column segments into capacity
      memory on demand. The gold model at roughly 900 GB across all layers, with an active
      working set well under that, fits F64 memory limits with margin; F32 would risk fallback
      to DirectQuery on wide queries, which would silently degrade the response times committed
      in Section 4.5.
   4. The streaming workload runs continuously. Eventstream and Eventhouse consume capacity
      around the clock, unlike the batch and interactive workloads. That baseline consumption
      must be subtracted before assessing peak headroom, and it is what pushes a marginal F32
      case over the line.
   5. Non-production environments do not need production capacity. Development at F8 and test
      at F16 are sized for functional correctness and, in test, for a proportional performance
      signal. Both are paused outside working hours, which is discussed under cost management
      below.

4.3 Cost Management
-------------------

  * Reserve the production F64 for one year, which reduces the rate materially against
    pay-as-you-go for a workload that runs continuously.
  * Run development and test as pay-as-you-go and pause them automatically outside 07:00–19:00
    on weekdays. A paused capacity accrues no compute charge, and the pause and resume are
    scripted through the Azure Resource Manager API as part of the same automation used for
    scheduled scaling.
  * Budget OneLake storage separately from capacity. Storage is billed on volume, not on SKU,
    so retention policy is a cost lever independent of capacity sizing — moving bronze to cool
    tier after thirteen months is a meaningful saving at 2.6 TB.
  * Charge back to hospital sites on report consumption metrics rather than splitting the
    capacity cost evenly, so that sites see the relationship between usage and cost.

4.4 Autoscaling Strategy
------------------------

Fabric capacity does not scale continuously on demand in the way an application tier does. The
strategy therefore combines four mechanisms — two built into the platform, two implemented by
the network — to absorb the Monday peak without paying for F128 for one hundred and
sixty-eight hours a week.


4.4.1 Mechanism 1 — Platform smoothing and bursting (built in)

Fabric smooths background operations across a twenty-four-hour window and interactive
operations across a five-minute window, and allows short bursts above the purchased capacity
level to be repaid from the smoothed budget. In practice this means the nightly Spark load
does not need to fit inside 64 CU at any single instant; it needs to fit inside the daily
budget. No configuration is required, but the behaviour must be understood, because it is why
the batch window in Section 4.2.2 consumes so little of the headroom that matters.


4.4.2 Mechanism 2 — Scheduled vertical scaling (implemented)

An Azure Automation runbook, triggered on a schedule, calls the Fabric capacity management API
to change the SKU. The scale operation is online and takes effect within a few minutes.


Schedule                 | Action                 | Reason
-------------------------+------------------------+-----------------------------------------
Monday 05:30 → F128      | Scale up               | Weekend review peak; the heaviest
                         |                        | interactive load of the week
Monday 11:00 → F64       | Scale down             | Peak has passed; demand returns to
                         |                        | typical weekday levels
Month-end close, 07:00 → | Scale up               | Board and service-line reporting cycle,
F128                     |                        | three business days
Month-end close +3 days  | Scale down             | Cycle complete
→ F64                    |                        |
Dev / Test 19:00 →       | Pause                  | No development activity overnight or at
paused                   |                        | weekends
Dev / Test 07:00         | Resume                 | Working hours
weekdays → resumed       |                        |

Scale-down is never automatic while a long-running job is in flight; the runbook checks for
active Spark sessions and pipeline runs and defers by fifteen minutes, up to three attempts,
before alerting rather than interrupting a load.


4.4.3 Mechanism 3 — Reactive scaling on sustained pressure (implemented)

The scheduled pattern handles known peaks. Unknown peaks — an unplanned surge, a poorly
written report published by an analyst, a mass-casualty event driving unusual dashboard
traffic — are handled reactively. An alert on sustained capacity utilization above eighty-five
percent for fifteen minutes triggers the same runbook to scale to the next SKU and notify the
on-call engineer, who then investigates the cause. Automatic scale-down follows two hours
after utilization returns below sixty percent, subject to the same in-flight-job check.


4.4.4 Mechanism 4 — Surge protection and workload isolation (configured)

Surge protection is enabled on the production capacity so that background operations are
rejected once background utilization crosses a defined threshold, protecting interactive users
from being throttled by a runaway batch job. In addition, the sandbox workspace where analysts
explore de-identified data is assigned to the development capacity rather than production, so
exploratory work cannot consume capacity that clinical users depend on. This is a deliberate
architectural choice: the cheapest form of autoscaling is preventing the load in the first
place.

4.5.1 Engineering practices supporting these targets

  * V-Order and OPTIMIZE with compaction run after every gold load, so Direct Lake reads a
    small number of well-sized row groups. Small-file proliferation is the most common cause
    of Direct Lake performance decay.
  * Gold facts are partitioned by discharge date and census date; date-filtered queries —
    which is nearly all of them — prune at the file level.
  * The agg_kpi_dept_day table backs the highest-traffic report pages so that the executive
    summary page never scans the encounter fact.
  * Report design standards limit each page to eight visuals, prohibit table visuals over ten
    thousand rows, and require that default slicer state restrict to the trailing thirteen
    months.
  * Every report is profiled with Performance Analyzer before promotion to production, with
    results attached to the pull request; a page exceeding the p95 target is not promoted.

5.2 Git Branching Strategy
--------------------------

A feature-branch workflow on a single long-lived branch. The repository is hosted in Azure

5.2.1 Rules

--------------------------

applied by a Tabular Editor script over XMLA rather than by hand. Report deployment uses the

5.8 Change Management in a Clinical Environment
-----------------------------------------------

  * Standard changes — new report page, new measure that does not alter an existing
    definition, cosmetic changes — follow the normal pipeline with the deployment window.
  * Significant changes — any change to a published KPI definition, to the security model, or
    to gold table schema — require change advisory board approval and advance notice to
    affected clinical leadership, because a leader who sees their length-of-stay figure move
    overnight without explanation will lose trust in the platform regardless of whether the
    new figure is more correct.
  * Emergency changes — a defect producing clinically misleading output, or an access control
    failure — may bypass the window under the hotfix path, with retrospective approval within
    one business day and a mandatory post-incident review. An access control failure is
    contained first and fixed second: revoke access, then diagnose.
  * Rollback is by redeploying the previous tagged release through the same pipeline. Every
    production deployment is tagged in Git with the release identifier and the change record
    number, so rollback is a documented, tested path rather than an improvisation at 22:30.

6.2.2 Runbook — nightly pipeline fails at 03:00

This is the scenario the on-call rotation exists for, and it is worth walking through
concretely because the decisions are time-boxed against the 06:00 clinical deadline.

   1. 03:00 — Detection. The pipeline On Failure activity writes to the operational log, posts
      to the data engineering Teams channel, and raises a PagerDuty incident. Activator
      independently monitors freshness, so a pipeline that hangs without failing is still
      detected.
   2. 03:05 — Acknowledgement. The on-call data engineer acknowledges within the fifteen-minute
      target. Failure to acknowledge escalates automatically to the secondary on-call at
      fifteen minutes and to the analytics lead at thirty.
   3. 03:05–03:20 — Triage. The engineer identifies the failed activity and classifies the
      cause into one of four buckets: source unavailable (EHR maintenance window overran,
      credential expired, network path down); data quality (schema drift, unmapped codes above
      threshold, referential failure); capacity (throttling, Spark session exhaustion); or
      platform (Fabric service incident, checked against the service health dashboard).
   4. 03:20 — Decision point. If the failure is transient, the engineer reruns from the failed
      activity; the pipeline is idempotent and restartable at activity granularity, which is
      why it is built that way. If the rerun can complete before 05:30, the 06:00 SLA is still
      met and the incident closes as Sev 2. If it cannot, the incident is raised to Sev 1 and
      step 5 applies.
   5. 04:00 — Partial publication. Where only some hospitals failed, the pipeline publishes the
      successful sites and marks the failed sites as stale in a data-status table that every
      report surfaces as a banner. Publishing eleven of twelve hospitals with a visible warning
      is better than publishing nothing, and far better than publishing twelve hospitals of
      which one is silently wrong.
   6. 05:30 — Communication. If the 06:00 SLA will be missed, the on-call engineer notifies the
      clinical operations duty manager and posts to the clinical leadership channel with a
      plain statement of what is stale, which sites are affected, and the expected recovery
      time. Communication is not optional and is not delegated to the recovery being finished.
   7. 08:00 — Handover. Unresolved incidents transfer to the day team at the morning stand-up
      with a written handover: what happened, what has been tried, current state, next action.
   8. Within 5 business days — Post-incident review. Sev 1 and repeated Sev 2 incidents receive
      a blameless review producing a tracked action. Recurring causes drive automation: three
      occurrences of the same failure mode is treated as a defect in the pipeline, not in the
      on-call rotation.

6.4 Business Continuity
-----------------------

The platform is an operational reporting system, not a clinical system of record. Its
unavailability does not affect patient care directly, and the recovery objectives reflect that
proportionality: a recovery time objective of four hours during business hours and a recovery
point objective of one business day, since the authoritative clinical data remains in the EHR
and can be re-extracted.

  * Item definitions are recoverable from Git; a workspace can be rebuilt from the repository
    and the deployment pipeline in under two hours, which is verified by the annual exercise.
  * Gold data is recoverable by re-running the transformation notebooks from bronze; bronze is
    recoverable by re-extracting from the EHR within the source retention window.
  * During an extended outage, hospitals fall back to EHR-native operational reports. This
    fallback is documented in the department runbooks so that it is a known procedure rather
    than an improvisation.
